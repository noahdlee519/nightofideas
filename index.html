Being a catalogue of original works of argument & speculation by living members of the University of Chicago, presented by night.

Every quarter, six students give lightning talks on an original idea, philosophical or otherwise, after weeks of workshopping with our reviewing team. Sixty or more people come to listen. Dinner is always served.

Past talks

Five works from recent Nights, hung frame to frame. Drag the wall, use the arrows, or choose from the catalogue below. Each frame opens its slides.

1. Pryma (Reilly)

Meditations on Meditations on Moloch

Or, How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Madness

A reply to Scott Alexander’s essay on Moloch, the god of races to the bottom, by way of Ginsberg’s Howl, Socrates and Wittgenstein, and ending on Nietzsche’s charge that the will to a system is a lack of honesty.

2. Lopez Molina (Violeta)

Relational Space(s): Thinking with Bubbles

Against the solitary Cartesian thinker, and the self-care influencer who may be his heir, a case from Sloterdijk’s Spheres: subjectivity as two or more parties open to each other.

3. Rodriguez (Cece)

Being After God

The Nihilism Lecture

Heidegger’s history of Being read as a history of nihilism: from Plato’s two worlds, through Nietzsche and the death of God, to what there is left to do.

How a Night comes together

The rules of the Salon, more or less. One quarter, one jury, six talks, and a long table at the end of it.

  1. Article I. Anyone may submit.

    Students and professors from every department apply with an original idea, philosophical or otherwise. No philosophy background is required.

  2. Article II. The jury chooses six.

    Each quarter the host reads the applications and selects six students to present.

  3. Article III. The workshop.

    For several weeks, our reviewing team works with each presenter to workshop the idea and edit the talk until it is ready for the Night.

  4. Article IV. The Night.

    Later in the quarter, six lightning talks before sixty or more listeners from across the university, often alongside a talk from a faculty guest. Dinner, always.

Have an idea you can’t put down?

Applications for this quarter are open to students and professors from every department. Send us the idea. If you’re selected, we’ll spend the weeks before the Night shaping it with you.
